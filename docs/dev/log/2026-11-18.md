# 2026-11-18

## MCP fixes from a blind test of every tool (`experimental`)

A tester that only saw the MCP server (descriptions, schemas, instructions) called every tool. Fixes:

- **Hollow crashed the app** on a plain 30 mm cube with a 2 mm wall (also from the UI's Hollow tool). Root cause: manifold-rust's `level_set` sizes its first hash table for a sparse surface (10·√cells) and, when it fills, resizes from the position of the last vertex that snapped to the surface; with none it panics ("table full before any vert", `sdf.rs:748`). A grid point snaps only when it lies within a quarter cell of the surface. A box's inner walls are big flat faces parallel to the grid, all at the same fraction of a cell; when that fraction is between 1/4 and 3/4 no point snaps, the crossings of the first face fill the table and it panics. 15 of 51 cube/wall combinations (12–60 mm, walls 1–3 mm) crashed. `solid::level_set` now meshes on whole cells from a corner shifted by 0, ½ or ¼ cell, trying the next shift when the library panics; between them every flat face lies near grid points in one. Regression test: cubes of 30/27/45/57 mm hollow to a closed solid with a cavity between the wall and the wall + 2 mm.
- **Panics no longer close the app**: `buildcraft3d::no_panic` turns a panic into an error. `modify_selected` (Hollow, Smooth, Extrude down… from the UI and the agent) runs each object's operation through it, so a failure is a notice and the scene stays as it was; every agent call runs through it too and returns a tool error. The default panic hook still prints the message (and a backtrace with `RUST_BACKTRACE`) to stderr.
- **Split** with a plane that misses a selected object deleted it (keep top below the object) or replaced it with a copy. `cut::misses` checks the plane against each object; `Scene::cut_pieces` now fails with "z 200 is above the top of the object (29.97 mm); nothing to cut" (or the bottom, or "the plane misses … which lies wholly below it" for a slanted plane), changing nothing. The UI's Split shows the same notice. Pieces keep the object's material.
- **measure** with no arguments and nothing selected returns the usual "nothing is selected" error (`mcp::nothing_selected`, shared with the other selection tools).
- **paint_area**: coverage is the largest bend between neighbouring faces the paint crosses (coverage × 180°), so on a smooth sphere every edge bends a few degrees and a small coverage floods it. Same as the UI's Coverage slider, which is unchanged. New `radius` (mm): only triangles whose centre is within it of `point` (Euclidean, the paint still spreads only through connected triangles; `Mesh::face_region_within`). The description now says this. The texture brush's coverage has the same behaviour and no radius yet.
- **get_scene** lists per object `material` (none/simple/realistic/translucent, `Material::name`), and `painted`, `textured`, `stickers`. `set_material`'s description is exact: any type but none sets the colour and clears painted areas; textures and stickers stay; none only removes the material.
- **Unknown or mistyped arguments** are rejected for every tool in one place, `mcp::check_args`, from the tool's own `inputSchema` (also nested objects such as `custom`, and `oneOf`). Run in `serve_agent`, so MCP clients and the in-app chat get it. Errors list the tool's arguments ("unknown argument units: transform takes back, bottom, front, …") or the type with the unit from the description ("size must be [x, y, z] in mm, three numbers; for cm multiply by 10").
- **edit** gains `group` and `ungroup` (the app's Group/Ungroup: one object without merging shapes; one object per separate solid). Ungroup checks `Mesh::solids`, so a hollowed object (outer shell plus cavity) is not "several solids".
- Wording: split/open/import say ids change; open clears the undo history and selects what it opened; insert_shape/import/load_image land at the centre of the floor and may overlap; load_image's 0.6 mm base (depth 3 = 3.6 mm tall, not for edge); spherical emboss is raised lettering, a sticker is a flat picture; extrude_outline no longer mentions keychains; smooth keeps about its size; measure's gap is 0 where boxes touch or overlap; screenshot's window picture includes open dialogs; GIF/BMP/WebP/TIFF say "isn't supported; convert to PNG" (`picture::unsupported`), and import of one gets the load_image hint; `file_name` without content and emboss_text with several objects selected have their own errors.

Noticed, not changed: the UI's Ungroup button is enabled for a hollowed object (`can_ungroup` counts mesh components, which include the cavity) and does nothing.

- `mcp::check_args` also rejects a value outside a parameter's `enum` and lists the
  choices, before the tool runs (an unknown `edit` operation used to report "nothing is
  selected" first).

## Measure: radius and snapping (`experimental`)

- **Radius**: the Measure tool has a mode list: Distance, Radius of a surface (a click on a curved face fits a cylinder or a sphere to the face's triangles, up to 30° between neighbours; flat or non-round faces say "Not a round surface") and Radius from 3 points (the circle through them, live with the cursor as the last point). The tool bar shows Radius and Diameter in the scene units; the viewport draws the fitted circle (three for a sphere) and a radius line in the same faint-wide plus thin style as the distance line. Model in `src/measure.rs`.
- **Snap**: the existing Snap switch (on by default) now snaps picked points to the vertex, edge middle, edge or face centre of the flat face under the cursor within 14 px (converted to mm at that depth), else keeps the point on the face plane; the floor is a plane too. A yellow marker with its name shows what was caught (square vertex, triangle midpoint, diamond edge, circle face centre, cross plane). The second point of a distance is lined up with the first: perpendicular distance when both lie on parallel faces (or the floor), exact along an axis within 3°. Holding Alt turns the Snap setting around while measuring. The original's Measure only has a Snap toggle (H54); the marker shapes and Alt are ours.
- **MCP / assistant**: `measure` gains `radius_of` (object id: sphere, cylinder, cone/torus widest circle), `circle` (3 points), and `snap`, `snap_tolerance` (mm, default 1) and `align` for from/to/circle; the assistant uses the same tool list. `--measure` for screenshots takes `[distance|surface|circle:]x,y,z;x,y,z;~x,y,z@vertex` (the `@kind` only sets the marker; surface fits the face nearest the first point).
- Not verified by hand: the click path with a real mouse (hover and click through `measure_spot`), and the screenshot of the new overlay in this environment.

## Shading off the UI thread (`experimental`)

- `scene::StatsCache` became the generic `WorkCache<T>`; `cached_stats` and the renderer use it. `Renderer::sync_mesh` uploads `Mesh::provisional_shading` for a new mesh and replaces it with the worker's `Mesh::shading` when ready (repaint requested through `FrameCallback::repaint`). UI-thread cost per new 320k-triangle mesh: 73 ms before, 2.1 ms after; final shading arrives after about 70 ms. Tests: worker result equals the synchronous shading, a superseded mesh's result is discarded.
- Not verified: the GPU upload path in a running window (the screenshot run exits with code 2 in this environment); the second upload of the final vertex buffers still happens on the UI thread.
- Removed the unused `sys-locale` dependency (no code, build script or packaging used it).

## Measure: circular edges (`experimental`)

- **Mode "Circular edge"** (fourth in Measure's list): hover highlights the edge loop under the cursor (yellow, like the snap indicator), a click chooses it (green); the bar shows Radius, Diameter and Center (scene units) and the viewport draws the fitted circle, the diameter line to the loop's first vertex, and the centre as a dot with a small cross. The centre cross and the Center field now show for "Radius of a surface" and "Radius from 3 points" too. If the loop is not round the bar says "Not a circular edge". Edge mode places no points (`capacity` 0), so the arrow cursor is not replaced.
- **Model** (`src/edge_loops.rs`): `FeatureEdges::compute(mesh)` welds vertices (`Mesh::welded_indices`, factored out of `face_region_within`), scans every edge once and keeps feature edges: open (1 face), non-manifold (more than 2) or faces bent more than 40°. At each feature vertex the edges are paired by smallest turn (at most 150°), so each edge is in exactly one chain (closed loop or open arc) whatever edge it is reached from; a coarse cylinder's side creases do not break its rim (45° turn along the rim against 90° onto the side edge). `EdgeLoop` fits a circle (`measure::fit_circle_around` in the plane found by Newell's normal) and reports RMS and worst deviation as fractions of the radius, sweep in degrees, vertex count.
- **Rule for "circular"**: RMS ≤ 1 % and worst ≤ 2.5 % of the radius; a closed loop needs 8 vertices or more (a square or hexagon has all corners on a circle but is a polygon; an octagon prism's rims count), an open arc needs 5 vertices and 40°. `EdgeLoop::why_not` gives the reason (also in the MCP error). Sphere: no creases, so no loops. 
- **Speed**: the UI keeps the feature edges per mesh in a `WorkCache` inside `Measure` (kept when the mode changes; repaint on completion, until then nothing is under the cursor); the loop under the cursor is cached by (object, chain, mesh, placement). The MCP side uses `edge_loops::feature_edges`, a thread-local cache of the last four meshes. Release timings (`circular_edge_latency` in `tests/perf_bench.rs`, ignored): 320k-triangle sphere 18 ms (no creases); camel 1.39M triangles 125 ms to find the edges (81k feature edges, 29k chains), then 0.5 ms for the nearest edge under the cursor (linear scan of the feature edges) and 0.01 ms to extract and fit its loop, 4 ms for all loops; flower pots of 0.4 to 1.3M triangles 26 to 106 ms and 0.1 ms per hover. Before: there was no way to get a circular edge but three clicks; the click-to-result for the 3-point circle is instant but manual.
- **MCP / assistant**: `measure` takes `edge_loop: {id, near}` (the circular edge nearest to the point: kind "circular edge", centre, axis, radius, diameter, fit_error as a fraction, fit_error_mm, vertices, closed, sweep_degrees, start, distance_to_edge; an error that says why when the nearest edge is not round, or that the object has no sharp edge) and `edge_loops: id` (all circular edges, largest first, at most 50, with `total`). `--measure edge:x,y,z` picks the loop nearest to the point for screenshots.
- Pots checked through the library API (`/run/media/nico/KODAK/3D/Macetas/Robert/`, 7 models, the test skips missing files): round loops are found on every model (8 to 24 each, mostly 4 to 100 mm). I could not tell which is "the rim" without the user: e.g. Meditación gives circles of diameter 64.7, 47.7 and 20.5 mm with vertical axes; Cafe a 23.5 mm circle at z = 0 and 13.9 mm ones. Rounded (filleted) rims with no crease above 40° are not feature edges and are not found; the UI's 3-point radius still works there.
- Not done: making a snap that lands on a circular edge switch to this result (the snap works on flat faces' outlines; mixing them was not natural). Not verified: the hover and click with a real mouse, the screenshot of the overlay (`--screenshot` hangs here), the MCP over the running Flatpak.

## Measure: rounded rims, soft edges and sections (`experimental`)

Problem: the pots' mouth has a smooth rounded rim (no crease over 40°), so "Circular edge" did not find it (Meditación: 19 loops, none at the mouth; `edge_loop near [0,0,99]` said not circular). Two ways added, both in the UI and the MCP `measure` tool, and compared (`tests/measure_models.rs`, ignored: `cargo test --offline --release -j 4 --test measure_models -- --ignored --nocapture`; the non-ignored tests there check generated shapes against their exact geometry).

- **A, soft edges** (`src/edge_loops.rs`): the crease angle is a parameter (`FeatureEdges::compute_at`, `compute_soft` = 6°, `feature_edges_at` caches per mesh and angle). MCP: `soft` and `edge_angle` on `measure` for `edge_loop`/`edge_loops` (`edge_loops` stays sharp unless asked; `edge_loop` tries sharp, and when no round sharp edge lies within 3 mm of the point (`NEAR_MM`) the soft pass, saying `soft: true`, `crease_angle` and a note; `soft: false` turns that off). UI: Circular edge hover/click falls back to the soft edges (their own `WorkCache`, only started when the sharp edge near the cursor is not round) and the bar adds "Soft edge (rounded rim)". A rounded rim is a ring of many close circles (one per facet row), so the nearest to the click is reported.
- **B, section** (`src/section.rs`, new; `cut.rs` only caps, it has no contour output): `section(mesh, model, point, normal)` slices with a plane, joins the crossings into contours, fits circles, nests them (outer/inner) and pairs round outer and inner contours into walls. MCP: `measure {section: {id, z | point+normal, near?}}` returns every contour (role, centre, axis, radius, diameter, fit_error, `why_not`), `walls` and `nearest`. UI: Measure mode "Section at height": a click cuts the clicked object horizontally at the clicked point's height (synchronously, 6 to 23 ms), draws all contours (yellow, the clicked one green) with the diameter line and centre cross, the bar shows Radius, Diameter, Center and Wall. `--measure section:x,y,z`.
- `primitives::revolve(profile, n)`: a solid of revolution (used by the tests to build pots with rounded rims).

### Results (release, see the table printed by the ignored test)

- Generated shapes (cylinder, cone, torus, hollow pots with a flat rim and rounded rims of 16 and 6 steps): both methods are exact where they apply. Sharp edges find the flat rim (80 and 74 mm), the cylinder and cone ends; a rounded rim (11° or 30° per facet) is invisible to the 40° pass; soft 6° finds it (rings from 77 up to 80 mm, the widest the true outer diameter) and the section gives 79.8/74.2 mm and the 3.00 mm wall at 1 mm below the top, 80.00/74.00 at the widest height. The torus has no sharp edges (soft gives 48 meaningless rings of its tube); the section at the tube centre gives 50.0 and 30.0 mm (band 10.0 mm). The cone's section at any height gives the exact diameter, which edges cannot.
- The 19 real pots (18 STL files under `Macetas/Robert` plus Madre e hijo): sharp edges find nothing at the mouth in any of them. Soft 6° finds 8 to 15 mouth rings in every one; the widest is 0.5 to 1 % under the section's outer diameter (e.g. 79.3 against 80.0 mm on Meditación medio, 109.1 against 110.0 on the grandes) because the widest facet row bends less than 6°; 3° gets 79.6 to 80.2 but finds more loops and some noise (Serio: a 0.85 % ring at the top). Sections scanned down from the top (1 mm steps) give a round outer contour in every file, fit error 0.00 to 0.2 %: 79.9 to 80.0 mm on most medios, 110.0 on most grandes, with the inner contour 72.6 (99.8) and the wall 3.4 to 5.1 mm; the exceptions are other pots (Mascotas 75.9, Serio 76.7 and 107.1, Lector acostado 81.3, Madre e hijo 102.0). The first round slice is 1 to 4 mm under the rim's top (the crown is too thin to be a circle). Centres from both methods agree to 0.5 mm.
- Latency: soft or sharp edges 27 to 160 ms per 0.4 to 1.3M-triangle mesh (once per mesh, on a worker in the UI), then 0.5 ms per hover; a section 6 to 23 ms per slice (welding the mesh dominates; no cache, done on the click).
- Verdict: for pots the section is better (exact diameter of both walls, centre, wall thickness, no ambiguity about which ring); soft edges are the way to pick the rim with a hover and see its outline, and work with no height; for cylinders and flat rims sharp edges are enough and exact; for cones and anything not bounded by an edge only the section works.
- Not verified by hand: the hover and click in the real window (hover fallback, section click, overlay drawing), the screenshot (`--screenshot` hangs here), the MCP over the running Flatpak. "Lector acostado" is probably lying on its side: its mouth circles are not about Z, so the Z-axis filter of the bench sees them only partly.
