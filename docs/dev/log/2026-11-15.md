# 2026-11-15

## SVG import and Extrude outline (`outline-svg`, from `experimental`)

- **SVG import** (`src/io/svg.rs`, `Format::Svg`, import only): usvg 0.45 (no text feature) resolves transforms, shapes, styles and units. Filled areas use their fill rule (even-odd or nonzero, holes kept); stroked paths become the band their stroke covers, with its joins and caps (clipper2's offset; dashes are drawn solid). Curves are flattened within 0.05 mm. One object per paint colour (gradients and patterns take the default colour); where shapes overlap, the later one wins, so objects do not overlap. 1 user unit = 25.4/96 mm, mm/cm/in sizes honoured; y is flipped so the drawing reads the same from above. Extruded 3 mm (`svg::HEIGHT`). SVG counts as a format with units: no units question. Text must be converted to paths.
- **Extrude outline** (Edit tab, after Extrude down; `src/outline.rs`, `src/app/outline.rs`): the selection's silhouette (manifold `project()`, or the union of triangle shadows for meshes that are not closed) offset with round corners. Outside = grown shape minus the silhouette (holes close in by the width); Inside = silhouette minus the shrunk shape. Width 2 mm, height the selection's, from its bottom. Keep model on with one object merges it with the border (`boolean::apply`, so painted faces keep their colours and the rest takes the object's); several objects, or a mesh that is not a closed solid, keep the border as a new object of the first one's colour, selected with them. Off: the border replaces the selection.
- The live preview runs on a job thread like Smooth's; the debounce/generation logic moved from `smooth.rs` into a shared `app/live.rs` (`Run<S>`). Apply is one undo step, Cancel restores the checkpoint.
- MCP: `extrude_outline` (side, width, height, keep_model); `open` and `import` mention SVG, and `import` ignores `units` for SVG. `--tool extrude-outline` for screenshots; icon: a star inside a rounded border.
- Clipper2 (`clipper2-rust`, already under manifold-rust) is now a direct dependency for SVG fill rules and strokes.
- Follow-up: the border's corners are sharp (miter joins, limit 4× the width) as in Tinkercad. Two sliders, Outer smoothing and Inner smoothing (default 0), round each edge on its own (open then close, radius up to twice the width); the inner edge keeps half the width clear of the outer one, so heavy rounding never breaks the border. The mm fields are typed text fields (also in Custom), Width stacked above Height. MCP: `smooth_outer`, `smooth_inner`.

## Emboss on any face, a tidier bar, and Conform (`emboss-surface`, from `experimental`)

- **Bug: emboss on a side face was refused.** Planar projection always sent rays straight down (-Z) from above the object, and dragging only set the pattern's X/Y offset. Dropping the pattern on a side face put its centre on the face's X/Y edge, so half the rays missed the object and the whole pattern was refused ("does not fit"). Planar now has a `facing` direction (`Emboss::facing`, default +Z): the pattern lies in the plane across it (upright on steep faces, up = +Y on flat ones, so it reads the right way round from outside) and rays go along -facing. Dragging takes the face under the pointer (`pick_hit`'s triangle, `emboss::place_on_placed`), `emboss::place`/`place_at` replace `offset_at`. The agent's `at` sets the face too. Tests: every face of a cube (raised and engraved), a slanted pyramid face.
- **Tool bar** (`src/app/emboss_bar.rs`, moved out of `tool_bar.rs`): two rows as Custom's. Row one: Pattern, Text (`style::text_field`), Font | Projection, Conform. Row two: Size, Depth (`style::unit_field`) | Bevel, Engrave (switches with their name, so their state shows) | Top, Sides colours (engraving hides them). Drop-downs are as wide as their longest choice (the font one as its chosen font), "Object color" became "Object". A gap separates each label-and-control pair; rows are field-high so labels line up.
- **Conform** (`src/emboss/surface.rs`, "Amoldar"): each projected point rises (or sinks) along the surface normal there, smoothed across triangle corners but not across edges sharper than 60°, instead of along the projection ray; walls, bevel, colours and the boolean are unchanged. It is a switch beside Projection rather than a fourth projection, because it changes which way the pattern grows, not how it reaches the surface: it combines with Planar, Cylindrical and Spherical. MCP `emboss_text` gets `conform`. Tests: on a sphere and on a cylinder side, raised and engraved, closed results and every vertex at depth (or the 0.5 mm overlap) from the surface.
- Emboss: the Spherical projection is gone; Planar (any face) and Cylindrical (wrap round, like a label) remain, and Conform works with both. Drop-down lists are as wide as their longest choice. Conform's direction is the faces within twice the depth averaged by area and nearness, so text bends smoothly over sharp edges.

## Emboss shows outlines and a card instead of a solid, and is cut at edges (`experimental`)

- **Preview** (`src/emboss/preview.rs`, `emboss::preview`): as in the original, the pattern is no longer previewed as an orange solid. `Preview` holds world-space polylines: the outlines where their rays land on the object (`Surface::hit`, split where they miss), the same outlines on the card they are projected from (`Frame::lift`, the plane or cylinder the rays start from, `card_gap` = 0.8 × size, at least 4 mm, past the object) and its border, and for Cylindrical a guide (rings at the card's top and bottom round the whole object, two upright lines). It reuses `pieces()`'s layout (`layout`, `turn`, `densify`) and rays. `Tool::Emboss::preview` stores it (cached by the same key); the viewport paints it with egui over the scene (`src/app/viewport/emboss.rs`): white 2 px outlines, pale green card, dimmer border and grey guide (`style::EMBOSS_*`).
- **Handles** for the bottom bar's mode, at `Preview::anchor` (centre on the surface, turned right/up, half size): Move, a green double arrow (dragging over the object still places it, Shift turns); Rotate, a thin circle round the pattern, dragged to turn `angle` (the pointer's angle round the centre in the pattern's plane); Scale, chevrons past the top right corner, dragged to scale `size` by the pointer's distance from the centre. `App::emboss_drag` is now `Option<EmbossDrag>` (`Place`, `Turn`, `Size`); the handle is grabbed where the button went down.
- **Edges**: a vertex whose ray misses the object no longer refuses the whole pattern. `clip` keeps only the top triangles whose corners all land and compacts their vertices; `Piece::solid` already walls the boundary of whatever top is left, so the clipped piece is still closed (resolution: the refine step). Pieces with nothing left are dropped; only when nothing at all lands is it an error ("The pattern does not fit on the object; move it onto the object"). The preview checks the same cheaply (any outline point landing, else the full build, for a pattern larger than the object). Same for Cylindrical (text past a cylinder's rim). Tests: outlines on a cube's top and the card above, the cylinder guide, text half past a top edge and a side edge (closed solid, larger volume), turned text over a corner raised and engraved, nothing on the object refused, a circle larger than the cube accepted.

## Emboss: move arrows, and X, Y and Z sizes in the bottom bar (`experimental`)

- **Move arrows** (`src/app/viewport/emboss.rs`): Move mode shows two double arrows like the object move gizmo, one past the pattern along its plane's right (the offset's X) and one along its up (Y), not turned with the pattern since the offset is not. They are built with `gizmo::handles` (new; `plane_arrow` now uses it) and painted and grabbed by `Gizmo::paint`/`grab`: blue, green when hovered, dragged or when their bar field is the current one, and the pointer becomes a hand. Dragging one (`EmbossDrag::Slide(axis)`) moves only along it: the pointer's movement projected on the arrow's screen direction, in mm (`Handle::amount`, as objects move); for Cylindrical X is along the surface. Picking a handle makes its bar field current, as for objects. Dragging elsewhere over the object still places the pattern freely.
- **Size model**: `Emboss` keeps `size` (the height: capital height for text, overall height for shapes and images) and gets `stretch`, the width as a multiple of the pattern's natural width at that size (1 by default, so the proportions are the pattern's own). `emboss::pattern_size` gives the laid-out width and height; `emboss::rescale(e, axis, ratio, lock)` scales width (0), height (1) or depth (2): locked, width and height move together (the height changes, the stretch stays); unlocked, the other stays in mm. The depth never follows the lock: it is how far the pattern stands out, not how big it is, so typing a width does not change the relief (the object lock scales all three; here only X and Y). Ranges as before: size 1–200 mm, depth 0.1–50 mm; stretch 0.05–20.
- **Bottom bar** (Scale): X, Y and Z fields (width in mm from `pattern_size`, size, depth), the underlined unit toggle (mm / %) and the lock, through `percent_toggle` and `lock_toggle`, now shared with the object bar. The % values compare `Emboss::extents` (size × stretch, size, depth) with the same when the tool opened (`Tool::Emboss::opened`), so 100 % is the opening size even if the text changes. The lock is on when the tool opens (`Tool::Emboss::lock`, `Tool::emboss` builds the tool); the unit choice is the objects' `scale_percent`. Size and Depth left the top bar.
- **Scale handles**: chevrons past the pattern's right and top edges size the width or height alone (both with the lock), a white dot past the corner sizes both, as the objects' face arrows and corner dots; each measures the pointer's distance from the centre along the turned pattern's axis.
- MCP `emboss_text`: `size` still sets the height with the width in proportion; a new optional `width` (mm) stretches it sideways. Tests: a stretched circle is 1.5× wider and as tall (outlines and solid), unlocked height keeps the width; the lock keeps the ratio and leaves the depth, and the ranges hold.

## Emboss handles are the object handles: turn ring, ghost and hands (`experimental`)

- **Rotate** (`src/app/viewport/emboss.rs`): the thin custom circle is gone. The pattern gets an object's turn ring, built by the same code: `gizmo::build`'s ring builder became `Gizmo::add_ring` over a `gizmo::Ring` (any centre, plane, radius and resting angle), and `gizmo::ring` makes a single loose one. The ring lies in the pattern's plane round it, with the object's arrow handle (`Kind::Turn`) resting on the pattern's left and turned with it. It is grabbed by the arrow only and dragged as objects are (`Handle::turned` round the ring's centre, frame by frame so a drag can go round; `Handle::amount` when the ring is edge on), snapping to 5° with Snap as objects do. While turning it shows where the arrow was when the drag began and the swept wedge to where it is now. `EmbossDrag::Turn` keeps the handle, the starting angle, the degrees turned, and the pointer's press and last positions. Grabbing it makes the angle field current, so it is green while dragging.
- **Cursor**: the open hand over an Emboss handle or over the selected object (where a press places the pattern), closed while the button is held and during any drag (place, slide, turn, size), through `icons::grab_cursor`, now shared with the object handles; `App::selection_under` is the hit test both use.
- MCP `emboss_text`: `width` gives a stretch clamped to 0.05–20 like `rescale` (`emboss::stretch_for_width`, measured from the natural width at stretch 1), so an extreme width cannot make a degenerate pattern. Tests: the loose ring's arrow and sweep, the width clamp.

## Emboss top bar like the original's (phase 3, `experimental`)

- **Layout** (`src/app/emboss_bar.rs`): row one is Pattern (still the drop-down; phase 4 brings the icon menu), Projection, the Bevel button and Color with its two buttons; row two is Text and Font (or the image's name), then our Conform and Engrave switches, which the original lacks. Groups are spaced rather than split by separators, as the original's.
- **Bevel** is a toggle button (`style::ribbon_button` at field height) with our own icon (`Icon::Bevel`, a block seen from above with sloped edges), lit while on.
- **Color**: a filled star for the top and an outline star for the sides (`Icon::StarFilled`, `Icon::StarOutline`), each with a chevron and the chosen colour as a line under its name (a dim line for the object's own). Each opens Paint's colour menu: Paint's menu code was split into `paint_menu::color_menu`, which takes its top rows (`MenuRow`: Eyedropper, Choose color, More colors, Use background color), the recent colours or none, and Material's tile style, and returns what was picked (`Picked`); `selected_color` (Paint, Material) now calls it. Emboss shows More colors…, "Use background color" (the object's own colour), recent and standard colours; a colour picked goes into the recent colours (`remember_color`). `Tool::Emboss::menu` is which button's menu is open. Engraving hides both buttons and closes the menu. Both menus are kept on screen.
- **Narrow windows**: when the bar has no room for the colour buttons' names they show the stars alone (names in tooltips), and the Text field narrows (70–120) so the switches after it fit; checked at 1366×768 with zoom 1.5.
- `--tool emboss-colors` opens Emboss with the top colour's menu, for screenshots.

## One standard flyout menu (`experimental`)

- **Builder** (`src/app/flyout.rs`): the dark flyout menus share one builder. `Flyout::new(id, at, width)` opens an area kept inside the window (egui's constrain, as before), `heights` sets its row and header heights, and `show` hands a `Menu` to fill from the top: `button_row(label, enabled)` (grey, lighter under the pointer, 2-point separator; true when clicked), `header(label)` (dark band), `tile_grid(count, &Grid, selected, paint)` (square tiles in columns with a pitch, painted by the caller, marked as a colour swatch or a picture; the index clicked) and `band(height, fill)` for contents of a menu's own. The menu's background and the click-elsewhere close (ignoring the opening click) are the builder's; `Shown::dismissed` reports it.
- **Users**: `paint_menu::color_menu` (Paint, Material, Emboss) builds its swatch list from it (`swatch_list`) and its "More colors" picker sits in a `Flyout` too; `texture_ui::menu::texture_menu` builds its Load, Texture picker and Camera rows, its QR block (a `band`), recent materials and the texture and stencil grids from it (rows 49 and headers 41 points, as before). The menu colours moved from `paint_menu` to `flyout`.
- Screenshots of `--tool paint-colors`, `texture-menu` and `emboss-colors` before and after: the texture menu is pixel-identical; the colour menus differ only in one anti-aliased pixel row at their bottom edge.

## Spherical projection is back (`experimental`)

- **Projection** (`src/emboss.rs`): `Projection::Spherical` returns: rays towards the object's bounding-box centre from a sphere round it, the pattern's offset being arc lengths along the circle of latitude and the meridian from the front (−Y). `Frame::normal`, `lift`, `key` and `tangents` handle it (right along the latitude, up along the meridian); the bins keep the seam opposite the pattern and give corners on the axis their neighbours' azimuth as for Cylindrical. Clipping at the object's edge, stretch, bevel and colours work unchanged.
- **Preview** (`src/emboss/preview.rs`): the card is lifted onto the sphere at the card's distance, so it curves round the object; the guide is a wire sphere like the original's: circles of latitude at the equator and ±50°, and four meridians 45° apart through both poles (`wire_sphere`).
- MCP `emboss_text` and the agent accept `spherical` again; "Spherical" is "Esférica". `--tool emboss-spherical` opens Emboss with it, for screenshots (`Action::EmbossPreset`).
- Tests: the restored unit and integration tests (text on a sphere's front 1 mm proud, near the pole, `place_at` round trip, vertical offset moves it up, engraving a star removes volume, now for both wrapped projections) and a preview test (seven guide circles on the card's sphere reaching both poles, a curved card in front, outlines on the surface, right and up at the front).
